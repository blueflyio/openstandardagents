# Academia and Educational Landscape (2025–2026)

## Cornell (eCornell): industry-facing agentic curriculum

Cornell’s Agentic AI Architecture certificate is structured as a progression from LLM fundamentals to RAG, then to tool-using agent architectures, and finally to strategy/governance/ethics. The sequence explicitly includes:

- hallucination/limitation understanding,
- RAG and GraphRAG patterns,
- multi-agent workflows and protocol concepts (including MCP),
- governance/risk/security and human oversight framing. [T09]

This is notable because it mirrors production concerns, not just model theory.

## Harvard: protocol-centric framing and governance-by-architecture

Harvard Library Innovation Lab’s Agent Protocols Tech Tree (APTT) and companion post frame open protocols as the strongest signal of practical consensus in a fast-moving ecosystem, with a direct analogy to early internet standardization (TCP/IP, SMTP, DNS, HTTP). [T10][T11]

The key policy claim in this body of work is that if “agents” are straightforward to assemble (model + loop + tools), then protocol design becomes a high-leverage governance surface. [T10]

Related Berkman short posts reinforce concern about large-scale agentic interactions and systemic risk, though these pages are brief and mostly point outward to other publications. [T71][T72]

## MIT ecosystem: deployment telemetry + management realism

### MIT AI Agent Index (2025)

The 2025 index (30 systems, 45 fields) documents:

- rapid feature deployment and autonomy increases,
- uneven transparency,
- sparse public safety-evaluation disclosure,
- unresolved norms for web conduct and agent accountability chains. [T12][T13][T14]

### MIT Sloan guidance (2026)

MIT Sloan materials consistently present a two-track message:

1. **Near-term caution**: agentic systems remain operationally risky (hallucinations, prompt-injection exposure, supervision tradeoffs). [T15][T16]
2. **Medium-term expectation**: high transaction-volume automation is plausible within a multi-year horizon. [T15]

MIT coverage of healthcare deployment studies also emphasizes sociotechnical implementation load (data, governance, integration, monitoring) over pure model tuning. [T18]

## HBR and broader management research signal

HBR’s 2025 discussion on agent supervision centers on autonomy calibration by uncertainty/risk understanding, not “max automation by default.” [T19]

## Additional research papers and cross-university contributions

- A survey of protocol interoperability (MCP/ACP/A2A/ANP) and staged adoption pathways. [T21]
- “Web of Agents” position paper arguing for minimal interoperability standards before fragmentation hardens. [T22]
- “Agentic Web” framing paper linking technical stack evolution with governance and socioeconomic implications. [T23]
- OpenID Foundation whitepaper (arXiv mirrored) on identity/authentication/authorization for agentic AI. [T24]
- MIT Media Lab’s delegated authority model based on OAuth 2.0 / OIDC extensions for auditable delegation chains. [T20]

## Practical conclusion from academia set

The strongest cross-source consensus is:

- **Interoperability standards are now core research and policy topics, not implementation details.**
- **Security and governance lag capability progress**, especially for identity, delegated authority, and auditable execution.
- **Deployment success depends as much on organizational control systems as on model quality.** [T13][T15][T18][T24]
