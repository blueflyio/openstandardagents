# Academia and Education Review (2025-2026)

Date compiled: March 27, 2026  
Relative-date normalization reference: March 10, 2026

## 1) Cornell: practical agent architecture + governance tracks

Cornell's eCornell "Agentic AI Architecture" certificate is notable because it explicitly links:

- LLM and prompt fundamentals
- RAG and context engineering
- Agent workflows/protocols (including MCP)
- Governance, risk, security, and human oversight

This is important: it treats agent engineering and governance as one curriculum, not separate tracks. [S54][S55][S56]

### Observed emphasis

- "AI that chats" -> "AI that acts"
- Multi-agent architecture patterns (routing, orchestration, reflection loops)
- Responsible deployment constraints (ethics, risk management, workforce effects)

## 2) Harvard: protocol-centric framing for the "agentic web"

Harvard's Library Innovation Lab (LIL) frames open protocols as the key lens for understanding fast-moving agent ecosystems. The argument is that when technologies are simple to replicate and distributed across many actors, protocol design often matters more than central mandates. [S16]

The LIL write-up also grounds this in internet history (TCP/IP, DNS, HTTP style coordination by rough consensus/running code), and extends the same logic to agent ecosystems. [S16]

Complementary Harvard policy/legal writing emphasizes institutional design questions:

- Who governs autonomous agent behavior online?
- Platform-controlled rules versus interoperable protocol governance
- Need for identity/delegation/accountability infrastructure

[S39]

## 3) MIT: deployment acceleration with transparency and safety gaps

MIT's 2025 AI Agent Index documents 30 deployed systems and 45 fields, and reports:

- Accelerating releases and autonomy
- Safety/transparency disclosure lagging capability disclosure
- No mature web-conduct norms for agents

[S17][S18]

MIT Sloan's 2026 guidance is directionally consistent:

- Agentic AI has strong business potential
- But major blockers remain (hallucinations, prompt injection, security controls)
- Human-in-the-loop will remain necessary in many production settings

[S19]

## 4) Additional research literature (open-access and practitioner-adjacent)

### Protocol and interoperability literature

ArXiv survey work in 2025 compares MCP, ACP, A2A, and ANP, and proposes staged adoption patterns depending on architecture maturity. [S46]

### Security and governance literature

Recent survey literature and practitioner synthesis converges on:

- Prompt injection as an architecture-level risk
- Tool/capability overreach as a primary failure mode
- Need for identity-aware authorization and runtime control

[S23][S24][S46]

### HBR and executive education layer

HBR 2026 pieces contribute management framing:

- Agent adoption is a work-design and governance problem, not only a tooling problem
- AI-era security cannot be treated as an extension of conventional app-only cyber controls

[S57][S58][S38]

## 5) Main academic signal for 2026

Across Cornell, Harvard, and MIT materials, the strongest shared message is:

1. Agent capability is progressing rapidly.
2. Governance/security standardization remains behind.
3. Protocols, identity, and operational controls are becoming the core infrastructure issue.
