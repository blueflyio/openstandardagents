# Academia and Education Findings (late-2025 to early-2026)

Date baseline used for relative references: **March 10, 2026**.

## 1) Cornell: applied training pipeline from LLM basics to governance

Cornell's eCornell **Agentic AI Architecture** certificate is a clear example of formal education moving from "prompting literacy" to "production-agent literacy." The program sequence explicitly covers:

- LLM behavior and hallucinations.
- RAG and context engineering.
- Agent architecture (tools, memory, workflows, protocol/handoff concepts including MCP).
- Governance/risk/security/human oversight in deployment. [T33]

This course design mirrors what engineering orgs are discovering in practice: deploying agent systems requires both software architecture skills and institutional governance skills, not only model experimentation.

## 2) Harvard ecosystem: protocol governance, institutional design, and web-order effects

### Berkman/LIL: protocols as governance lens

Harvard Library Innovation Lab's Agent Protocols Tech Tree (from a Berkman-centered workshop context) frames open protocols as the clearest indicator of what the builder community is standardizing in real time. It explicitly compares this moment to early internet protocol formation. [T34]

The strategic point is strong: where centralized regulation is immature or slow, protocol choices become de facto governance.

### Harvard legal/policy commentary

Harvard JOLT discussion on the "agentic web" emphasizes the institutional fight between platform-controlled authority and protocol-mediated interoperability, especially around delegation, identity, and accountability across platforms. [T43]

HKS policy writing further highlights traffic and infrastructure pressure from bot/agent growth and argues for:

- better bot-human differentiation mechanisms,
- protocol and network adaptation,
- and policy frameworks that assume imperfect safety rather than perfect prevention. [T44]

## 3) MIT and MIT-linked work: adoption, readiness gaps, and management implications

### MIT AI Agent Index

The MIT AI Agent Index (and companion paper) documents 30 deployed agents and highlights uneven transparency and safety disclosure patterns across the ecosystem, providing a baseline for policy and procurement analysis. [T35][T39]

### MIT Sloan / MIT SMR 2026 guidance

MIT Sloan and MIT SMR guidance converges on a "near-term caution, medium-term inevitability" stance:

- Agentic AI is advancing rapidly.
- Hallucinations and prompt-injection/security risks remain major blockers for fully autonomous high-stakes workflows.
- Human-in-the-loop remains necessary today.
- Yet agents are expected to absorb large portions of transactional work as controls mature. [T36][T37]

### MIT Initiative on the Digital Economy studies

MIT IDE-linked work extends this beyond opinion pieces to empirical and experimental studies (human-agent collaboration, negotiation, trust in AI search, exception handling behavior), indicating agent design outcomes are highly sensitive to interface design and governance choices. [T38]

## 4) Harvard Business Review and management scholarship signals

HBR and HBS-linked publishing in early 2026 introduces the emerging managerial role of **agent manager** (or equivalent), emphasizing observability, agent performance supervision, and hybrid workforce governance. Full HBR content is partially gated, but metadata/abstract channels are clear enough to track the management trend. [T45][T46]

## 5) Other academic literature (arXiv / security-oriented research)

Recent survey and analysis papers provide technical framing for protocol and security comparisons:

- interoperability survey of MCP/ACP/A2A/ANP,
- communication-security survey,
- comparative protocol security evaluation studies. [T40][T41][T42]

These sources generally agree that interoperability is increasing faster than shared security baselines.
