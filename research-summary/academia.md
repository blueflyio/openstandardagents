# Academia and Research Landscape (2025-2026)

This document summarizes university and research-center perspectives on autonomous/agentic AI, interoperability protocols, and governance/security.

## 1) Cornell (eCornell): curriculum-level framing of production agent systems

The eCornell Agentic AI Architecture certificate explicitly sequences learning from:

- LLM fundamentals and prompt/context engineering,
- RAG and grounding,
- tool/memory-based agent architectures and agentic protocols (including MCP),
- governance, risk, security, and human oversight for scaled deployment. [R07]

### Why this matters

This is one of the clearer institutional signals that "agentic AI" is being taught as a **socio-technical system** (architecture + governance), not a pure model feature. The program's language emphasizes responsible deployment and organizational controls, not only capability expansion. [R07]

## 2) Harvard: protocol-centric governance lens

Harvard Library Innovation Lab's Agent Protocols Tech Tree (APTT) argues that open protocols are a practical "x-ray" of what builder communities are standardizing, in the same way early internet protocols shaped network behavior through interoperability incentives. [R08]

Key Harvard framing points:

- agents are comparatively easy to assemble (model + loop + tools),
- centralized control is limited,
- protocols therefore become a major governance lever.

Related Harvard policy/legal commentary argues that the central institutional question is whether agentic systems are governed by platform terms or open interoperable protocol infrastructure. [R44]

### Why this matters

Harvard's work provides a governance interpretation useful for policy and standards strategy: protocol design decisions are de facto policy decisions.

## 3) MIT: rapid capability growth, weak safety transparency

### 3.1 MIT AI Agent Index 2025 (site + paper)

MIT's Index and supporting paper document 30 deployed agents and identify:

- fast release velocity (most entries launched/updated recently),
- increasing autonomy,
- disclosure asymmetry (capabilities more documented than safety),
- weak third-party safety testing transparency,
- unsettled norms for agent web behavior. [R09][R10][R48]

Notable institutional takeaway: **ecosystem transparency does not scale with autonomy**.

### 3.2 MIT Sloan and MIT SMR: "de-hype, operationalize, and govern"

MIT Sloan's decision guidance and MIT SMR's 2026 trend analysis converge on:

- continued value potential from agentic systems,
- current readiness limits due to reliability and security exposure (including prompt injection),
- need for stronger enterprise operating models (AI factories, organizational accountability, deployment discipline). [R11][R12]

### Why this matters

MIT signals that agentic AI should be treated as a deployment/governance challenge as much as a frontier-model challenge.

## 4) Cross-university and open research surveys

A 2025 arXiv survey compares MCP, ACP, A2A, and ANP by interaction style, discovery model, and security assumptions, and proposes staged adoption patterns rather than single-protocol lock-in. [R47]

The 2025 AI Agent Index paper (arXiv) further contributes a structured evidence approach for tracking deployed agent properties and transparency. [R48]

### Evidence pattern from these papers

- interoperability remains multi-protocol,
- practical deployment choices are contextual,
- governance visibility remains underdeveloped.

## 5) Governance and policy adjacencies (Harvard policy and bot-traffic framing)

Harvard-linked policy commentary and student policy analysis highlight macro risks from automation traffic scale, arguing for protocol and infrastructure upgrades and stronger human/bot differentiation mechanisms. [R44][R45]

These are not standards bodies, but they provide policy-oriented framing that matches the concerns documented in security and standards communities.

## 6) Synthesis for research objective #1

Academic and university-adjacent sources collectively suggest:

1. **Agentic AI is now treated as a systems discipline** (architecture + orchestration + policy).
2. **Open protocol literacy is becoming foundational** for understanding ecosystem direction.
3. **Transparency and accountability lag capability** in deployed systems.
4. **Security and governance controls are expected to become first-class curriculum and operational concerns**, not optional add-ons.

## 7) Coverage gaps and caveats

- HBR content relevant to organizational adoption is largely paywalled; only summaries were used. [R50]
- Several useful policy pieces are commentary rather than peer-reviewed empirical studies; they are included as context, not primary proof.
- The strongest empirical academic anchors in this set are the MIT Index data and the protocol survey papers. [R47][R48]
