# Academia and Research-Center Findings (2025-2026)

**Research reference date for relative-time normalization:** **March 10, 2026**.

## Cornell

The eCornell *Agentic AI Architecture* certificate is notable because it treats agentic systems as a full lifecycle discipline rather than a prompt-engineering add-on. Public program descriptions connect LLM foundations to RAG, multi-agent workflows, protocol-level interoperability, and governance/ethics modules. This aligns with enterprise demand for agents that can act, retrieve, and comply in the same architecture. [T08]

## Harvard

Harvard Library Innovation Lab's Agent Protocols Tech Tree (APTT) frames protocol standardization as a structural governance mechanism, not only a developer convenience. The central argument is that open protocols expose where builder communities converge and can play the same institutional role that TCP/IP and DNS played for the web. [T09][T10]

Related Harvard policy/legal writing on the "agentic web" emphasizes institutional design questions: identity, verifiable delegation, and accountability in environments where autonomous traffic may grow rapidly. While these works are more policy-analytic than systems-technical, they are useful for governance framing. [T45][T46]

## MIT

MIT's AI Agent Index (2025 edition) highlights three core realities: rapid deployment, rising autonomy levels, and transparency deficits. The published detail pages report that many agents were updated in 2024-2025 while safety and third-party assurance disclosures remained limited, creating a difficult evidence environment for external evaluators. [T11][T12]

MIT Sloan's 2026 decision-maker guidance takes a cautious-optimistic position: current agent systems have serious reliability and security limits (including hallucination and prompt injection concerns), yet agent-mediated transactions are expected to scale materially through the next five years (normalized to 2031 from the article date context). [T13]

## Other Research and Scholarly Sources (Late 2025-Early 2026)

Security-focused arXiv work in 2025-2026 moves from ad hoc controls toward formalized models:

- A protocol threat-modeling study (submitted February 11, 2026) analyzes MCP, A2A, Agora, and ANP with a structured risk inventory and case-study lens. [T40]  
- AgentRFC (submitted March 25, 2026) proposes a layered Agent Protocol Stack, formal security principles, and conformance checking for composed protocol ecosystems. [T41]  
- The Aegis Protocol (submitted August 22, 2025) proposes layered security with DIDs, post-quantum cryptography primitives, and zero-knowledge policy-compliance proofs. [T42]

Together, these papers indicate that the research frontier has shifted from "how to build agents" to "how to assure multi-protocol agents under composition and adversarial pressure." [T40][T41][T42]

## University-Adjacent Governance Guidance

University and policy-adjacent materials converge on several controls:

1. Treat agents as first-class identities, not just sessions behind shared API keys. [T11][T38]  
2. Require policy-bounded action scopes and auditable delegation traces. [T38][T41]  
3. Preserve human-in-the-loop gates for high-impact actions until measured reliability stabilizes. [T13][T44]
