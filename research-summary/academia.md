# Academia and Education (Cornell, Harvard, MIT, and Adjacent Research)

Prepared: March 20, 2026

## 1) Cornell / eCornell

### Agentic AI Architecture program direction

eCornell's Agentic AI Architecture certificate is framed as a progression from LLM fundamentals to agent architecture, multi-agent workflows, and responsible deployment controls (governance/risk/security). [R36]  
Course-level pages explicitly emphasize tools, memory, and architecture patterns in production workflows. [R37]

### Why it matters

This is one of the clearest university-affiliated professional programs tying together:

- LLM fundamentals
- agent orchestration
- protocol-aware integration patterns
- governance and oversight readiness

This "architecture + governance together" framing aligns with current enterprise gaps in security approval and controls. [R40][R42]

## 2) Harvard ecosystem

### Agent Protocols Tech Tree (Berkman Klein + Harvard LIL)

Harvard Library Innovation Lab and Berkman Klein's Agent Protocols Tech Tree presents open protocol evolution as a "tech tree" to make interoperability and dependency paths legible. [R34][R35]  
The project argument is that open protocols reveal practical coordination choices by builders and can shape governance trajectories similarly to early internet protocols. [R34][R35]

### Governance and institutional concerns

Berkman Klein's 2026 governance piece argues that increasingly autonomous agentic systems require continuous, technology-assisted oversight rather than periodic ex-post controls. [R38]  
Harvard JOLT commentary on the "agentic web" highlights institutional conflict between platform-centric control and protocol-centric interoperability/accountability, using the Amazon-Perplexity dispute as a concrete case. [R39]

## 3) MIT

### MIT AI Agent Index (2025 cohort)

The MIT AI Agent Index and companion publication document rapid deployment and substantial transparency asymmetries:

- 30 deployed agents indexed
- acceleration in launches/major updates during 2024-2025
- materially lower disclosure of agentic safety evaluations versus capability claims [R29][R30][R31]

The index explicitly reports the absence of widely adopted norms for agent behavior on the open web, reinforcing the standards/governance gap. [R29][R30]

### MIT Sloan management guidance (2026)

MIT Sloan's 2026 guidance positions agentic AI as high-potential but not yet frictionless, citing issues such as hallucinations, security vulnerabilities, and the operational burden of maintaining human oversight. [R32][R33]  
This is consistent with the security data in industry reporting and with NIST's identity/authorization focus. [R40][R42]

## 4) Other research tracks (2025-2026)

### Governance/security preprints and applied frameworks

Recent arXiv activity includes runtime governance and agentic security architecture proposals (for example policy-on-paths and AI-RMF-aligned governance implementations). [R50][R51][R52]

### Management/policy publications

Harvard Business Review 2026 articles discuss new operating roles and organizational readiness for agentic deployment; however, full text is often paywalled. [R48]

## 5) Academic takeaways

1. **Technical capability is advancing faster than behavioral standards**. [R29][R31]  
2. **Governance is moving toward runtime controls**, not static policy documents. [R38][R42][R50]  
3. **Education programs are now explicitly integrating architecture + safety + governance**, indicating a shift from prototype building to operational stewardship. [R36][R37]
