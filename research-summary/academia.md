# Academia and education landscape (2025-2026)

## 1) Cornell / eCornell: practice-first agent architecture curriculum

eCornell’s **Agentic AI Architecture** certificate explicitly links foundational LLM behavior to production agent workflows. The course copy calls out: RAG, Model Context Protocol (MCP), multi-agent workflows, handoffs, and governance/risk/security/human oversight as required competencies, not optional extras. That is notable because it mirrors the same stack enterprises now deploy (tool protocols + orchestration + controls). [A1]

Practical reading: Cornell is framing agent engineering as **systems engineering + governance**, rather than prompt design alone. [A1]

## 2) Harvard (Library Innovation Lab): protocols as public infrastructure

Harvard LIL’s **Agent Protocols Tech Tree** (published 2026-02-23) makes a strong argument: open protocols reveal where communities have true working consensus, similar to early internet protocol formation. The post directly describes protocols as an “x-ray” of what builders agree on, and argues agent techniques are hard to regulate directly, so protocol design is a core governance lever. [A2][A3]

Practical reading: this is a governance thesis through technical standards, not only through policy documents. [A2]

## 3) MIT: measurement and deployment realism

### MIT AI Agent Index (2025 edition)

MIT’s index provides a structured evidence base over 30 agents and 45 annotation fields. In the “Further details” analysis:

- agent autonomy is rising quickly;
- safety transparency lags capability disclosure;
- only a minority of high-autonomy systems disclose meaningful safety evaluation details;
- web-conduct norms remain unsettled (including anti-bot bypass behavior in some browser agents). [A4][A5]

This is one of the strongest public baselines for comparing agent ecosystem maturity. [A4][A5]

### MIT Sloan / MIT SMR

MIT Sloan’s 2026 decision-maker guidance states that hallucinations and prompt-injection risk are still slowing confidence in fully autonomous deployment, while also forecasting that agents could handle most transactions in many large business processes within five years (i.e., by approximately 2031 from the 2026 statement). [A6]

MIT Sloan and MIT SMR also emphasize organizational tensions (autonomy vs supervision, speed vs redesign) and report significant enterprise adoption momentum. [A7][A8]

## 4) Harvard policy discussion on “agentic web” pressure

A Harvard policy-review source describes bots as already accounting for a large share of traffic and frames agent growth as an internet governance challenge, arguing for standards/protocol thinking rather than only reactive control. [A9]

## 5) Synthesis: what academia is converging on

Across Cornell, Harvard, and MIT, the convergence is clear:

1. **Agent design is easy; safe deployment is hard.**
2. **Open protocols are becoming policy instruments** (de facto governance).
3. **Measurement and disclosure lag deployment speed**, especially for security and autonomy claims.
4. **Human oversight and explicit boundaries** remain central in near-term production.
