# Academia and Education Review (2025-2026)

## 1) Cornell: curriculum signals for enterprise agent architecture

The eCornell Agentic AI Architecture certificate is notable because it combines hands-on agent implementation (tools/memory/patterns) with RAG and governance topics in one structured pathway. [S21]

Why this matters:
- It mirrors enterprise reality: successful deployments require both engineering patterns and governance controls.
- It also indicates that "agent architecture" has moved from ad-hoc experimentation into formal executive/professional education.

## 2) Harvard: protocols as institutional infrastructure

Harvard Library Innovation Lab’s Agent Protocols Tech Tree (APTT) frames protocols as the infrastructure layer that shapes what kinds of agents become easy to build and deploy, analogizing to earlier internet protocol eras. [S22][S23]

Key contribution:
- It re-centers the conversation away from model-only capabilities and toward institutional coordination mechanisms (shared language, interoperability norms, protocol governance). [S22]

Related policy framing in Harvard JOLT emphasizes that the "agentic web" is an institutional design problem, not only a technical one, especially around attribution, responsibility, and cross-platform behavior. [S28]

## 3) MIT: deployment reality and transparency gaps

MIT’s 2025 AI Agent Index provides a structured inventory of deployed systems and repeatedly highlights transparency and standards gaps (including weak disclosure around safety testing and web behavior norms). [S24][S25]

MIT Sloan’s 2026 decision-maker guidance is aligned with this caution: agentic AI capability is advancing rapidly, but reliability and security concerns (hallucinations, prompt-injection exposure) still block broad autonomous operation in sensitive settings. [S26][S27]

## 4) Other scholarly streams (HBR, arXiv, MIT Press)

### HBR / management research

HBR pieces in early 2026 discuss the emergence of operational roles for supervising AI agents and preventing "agent sprawl" in enterprises. Even when not deeply technical, they capture an important governance trend: agent systems need managerial and policy structures, not only model tuning. [S29]

### arXiv governance/security wave (late-2025 to early-2026)

Recent work highlights:
- concrete prompt-injection pathways in agent skill ecosystems, [S30]
- constitutional governance mechanisms for multi-agent collectives, [S31]
- layered execution-time control architectures for autonomous systems. [S32]

These papers are uneven in empirical maturity, but collectively show a shift from "can agents do tasks?" to "how do we constrain and audit autonomous action in production?"

### MIT Press context

MIT Press materials (for example, *Agents in the Long Game of AI*) provide broader conceptual framing around trustworthy and hybrid agent systems, useful as theory background even when not implementation-specific. [S33]

## 5) Academic consensus themes (as of 2026-03-17)

1. **Interoperability is now a first-order research concern**, not an implementation footnote. [S22][S24]
2. **Transparency and evaluation lag capability release cycles.** [S24][S25]
3. **Security controls must be runtime-enforced** (authorization, containment, and auditability), not only documented. [S30][S32]
4. **Governance is socio-technical**: technical guardrails and institutional oversight both matter. [S26][S29]

## 6) Gaps and limitations in available literature

- Some high-impact guidance is in essays/blog formats rather than peer-reviewed venues.
- Comparative benchmark standards for cross-protocol agent behavior are still immature.
- "Web conduct standards for agents" remains an open problem noted in MIT index context and echoed by policy discussions. [S25][S28]
