# Academia and Research (2025-2026)

Last updated: April 17, 2026

## Cornell (eCornell): practical curriculum trajectory

Cornell’s public course material for “Building AI Agents With Tools, Memory, and Agentic Architecture” frames the educational arc from LLM basics to autonomous workflows, emphasizing:

- model + prompt + tools + memory as core agent components,
- architecture patterns (prompt chaining, routing, orchestrator-worker, reflection),
- protocol-level interoperability and handoffs,
- MCP as a practical integration standard [SRC-015].

This aligns with industry needs: teams are expected to move from “single prompt” systems toward composable, tool-using orchestration with explicit controls.

## Harvard: protocols as institutional infrastructure

Harvard Library Innovation Lab’s Agent Protocols Tech Tree argues that open protocols reveal where builder consensus is forming and can shape how AI agents behave online, similar to how foundational Internet protocols shaped early web evolution [SRC-016].

The central claim is that agents are easy to reproduce (model + tools + loop), so policy and governance outcomes will be significantly influenced by protocol design choices rather than centralized platform control alone [SRC-016].

Complementing this, Harvard JOLT commentary (2026) frames the “agentic web” as a governance fork between:

- platform-mediated gatekeeping, and
- protocol-mediated identity/delegation/accountability [SRC-035].

## MIT: deployment acceleration and transparency gaps

MIT’s 2025 AI Agent Index and its methodology pages identify key ecosystem conditions:

- rapid release cadence and rising autonomy,
- incomplete disclosure of safety testing,
- limited third-party evaluation visibility,
- unresolved standards for agent web conduct [SRC-012][SRC-013][SRC-038].

MIT Sloan’s 2026 guidance similarly warns that agentic AI is not yet ready for broad unsupervised deployment due to hallucination risk and prompt-injection vulnerability, while still forecasting material near-term enterprise penetration [SRC-014].

## MIT Media Lab and adjacent governance research

MIT Media Lab’s “Authenticated Delegation and Authorized AI Agents” proposes extending OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect with agent-specific credentials/metadata and auditable delegation constraints [SRC-036]. This work is directly relevant to enterprise concerns around “on-behalf-of” execution, least privilege, and accountability chains.

## Cross-university pattern

Across Cornell, Harvard, and MIT sources, the shared pattern is:

1. **Agent capability is growing faster than institution-grade control.**
2. **Protocol literacy is becoming as important as model literacy.**
3. **Identity, delegation, and auditability are the center of governance research.**
