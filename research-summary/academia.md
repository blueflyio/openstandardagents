# Academia and Education Signals (late-2025 to early-2026)

This section tracks how universities and public-interest institutions are shaping the agentic AI agenda through teaching, protocol mapping, risk analysis, and policy research.

## 1) University and policy landscape

### Cornell (professional education)
Cornell's Agentic AI Architecture certificate emphasizes practical architecture topics: LLM fundamentals, RAG patterns, multi-agent workflows, MCP integration, and governance/ethics topics for decision-makers. This is a market signal that "agent engineering" has moved from research-only into structured workforce training. `[T12]`

### Harvard (protocol literacy and governance)
Harvard's Agent Protocols Tech Tree (APTT) frames protocols as "infrastructure choices" that shape system behavior, lock-in risk, and governance boundaries. Harvard JOLT's policy analysis on the agentic web similarly stresses institutional design, standards governance, and identity accountability ("know your agent"). `[T13][T21]`

### MIT (benchmarking and executive guidance)
The MIT AI Agent Index 2025 highlights transparency and accountability deficits, especially around web conduct standards and provenance. MIT Sloan's 2026 executive guidance states agentic systems are strategically important but still vulnerable to hallucination and prompt injection in production-critical workflows. `[T14][T15]`

### NIST/NCCoE (federal identity architecture)
NIST NCCoE's concept paper (February 5, 2026) focuses on applying mature identity and access standards to software and AI agents, indicating that formal IAM patterns (credentials, attestation, policy, auditing) are becoming baseline expectations for enterprise and regulated deployments. `[T16]`

## 3) Teaching themes that match production reality

Across Cornell/Harvard/MIT/NIST material, five recurrent themes emerge:

1. **Interoperability first**: protocol choices now determine long-term extensibility. `[T13]`
2. **Identity before autonomy**: autonomous behavior without verifiable identity is unacceptable in high-trust systems. `[T16][T21]`
3. **Observability as governance**: tracing and explainability are treated as safety controls, not optional diagnostics. `[T14][T15]`
4. **Human-in-the-loop remains mandatory** for high-impact actions in near-term deployments. `[T15]`
5. **Security externalities are systemic**: protocol and tool design can amplify or reduce risk. `[T14][T16]`

## 4) Date-converted interpretation notes (using March 10, 2026 baseline)

- MIT Sloan's statement that widespread enterprise use is likely "within five years" implies a planning horizon through **2031**. `[T15]`
