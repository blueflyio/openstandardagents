# Academia and Research Institutions (2025-2026)

Date of synthesis: April 6, 2026.

## Cornell (eCornell): curriculum signal from education to practice

Cornell’s Agentic AI Architecture certificate is notable because it explicitly sequences:

1. LLM foundations and prompt/context engineering,
2. RAG and grounding,
3. tool/memory/agentic architecture (including MCP mention),
4. strategy, governance, ethics, risk, and oversight.[T30]

This is a strong indicator that “agent engineering” is already being taught as a full-stack discipline (technical + organizational governance), not just prompt design.

## Harvard: protocols as governance levers

### Harvard Library Innovation Lab (LIL)

The Agent Protocols Tech Tree project frames open protocols as a practical lens for understanding where agent ecosystems are converging.[T24][T25]  
The central claim is that protocols reveal de facto consensus in fast-moving ecosystems more quickly than formal regulation can.[T24]

### Harvard policy/legal commentary

Two Harvard-adjacent policy/legal sources are relevant:

- HKS Student Policy Review article discussing super-linear agent traffic growth risks and protocol upgrades for bot-bot traffic management.[T31]
- Harvard JOLT commentary on institutional governance of the “agentic web” (platform control vs open protocol control), emphasizing identity, delegation, and accountability infrastructure.[T32]

These are not equivalent to peer-reviewed empirical studies, but they provide useful governance framing.

## MIT: empirical baseline plus deployment caution

### MIT AI Agent Index (2025)

The MIT AI Agent Index is one of the strongest public snapshots of deployed agent ecosystems:

- 24/30 prominent agents launched or significantly updated in 2024-2025.
- Transparency gap: only 4/13 frontier-autonomy agents disclose agentic safety evaluations.
- Web-conduct standards remain unsettled.
- Ecosystem concentration around GPT/Claude/Gemini model families.[T26][T27]

### MIT Sloan guidance (2026)

MIT Sloan’s 2026 guidance is explicit: agentic AI is strategically important but still constrained by hallucination and prompt-injection risk, so “human in the loop” remains necessary for many workflows.[T28]

### MIT CISR governance research

MIT CISR’s “Agents of Change” project centers governance questions about decision rights, multi-agent oversight, and responsible experimentation in enterprise settings, indicating that governance design is now an active institutional research area.[T59]

## HBR, MIT Press, ACL/arXiv, and related research corpus

### HBR (partially paywalled)

- “Is Your Workplace Set Up for AI Agents?” argues organizational redesign is required for meaningful gains from agents (not simple bolt-on automation).[T50]
- “AI Agents Act a Lot Like Malware” frames operational risk through cybersecurity containment patterns.[T51]

Because full text is limited, these are best interpreted as executive framing sources.

### MIT Press book context

“Agents in the Long Game of AI” (2024) argues for hybrid/cognitive architectures and explainability as foundations for trustworthy agents.[T60]  
It is not a 2026 empirical benchmark, but useful for architectural context.

### Security research trend (ACL/arXiv)

By late 2025 through early 2026, prompt injection and tool-chain attacks became a substantial research stream:

- AgentDojo benchmark for dynamic agent security evaluation.[T52]
- Multi-agent arbitrary malicious code execution study.[T53]
- Defense benchmarking and mitigation papers in RAG/agent settings.[T54]
- Systematization of coding-assistant vulnerabilities.[T55]
- Adaptive indirect prompt injection attack frameworks.[T56]
- Communication-layer attack research in multi-agent systems (AiTM).[T57]
- Offensive jailbreak framework targeting computer-use agents (SUDO).[T58]

## Practical takeaways from academia-focused sources

1. **Governance is now core curriculum and research**, not an add-on.
2. **Interoperability protocols are being treated as institutional infrastructure**.
3. **Security and transparency remain major unresolved gaps** despite rapid capability progress.
4. **Enterprises should assume staged autonomy with continuous oversight**, not immediate full autonomy at scale.
