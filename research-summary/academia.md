# Academia and Research Institutions (2025-2026)

Date of synthesis: April 15, 2026

## Cornell University (eCornell): practical architecture + governance framing

Cornell’s Agentic AI Architecture certificate explicitly sequences learning from LLM fundamentals and RAG to tool-enabled agents, multi-agent workflows, protocol concepts (including MCP), and then governance/risk/human-oversight considerations.[T16]  
This is notable because the curriculum does not present autonomy as “pure automation”; it frames deployment as an engineering + governance problem from the start.[T16]

## Harvard: open-protocol governance lens

Harvard Library Innovation Lab’s Agent Protocols Tech Tree (APTT) argues that open protocols expose where consensus is forming in emerging agent infrastructure, similar to internet-era protocol evolution.[T17][T18]  
The Berkman Klein Center framing highlights the same point: protocols are becoming policy-relevant governance surfaces, not only technical implementation details.[T18]

## MIT: transparency and readiness gap

MIT’s AI Agent Index (2025 edition and methodology notes) reports:

- rapid release velocity of highly agentic systems,
- weak safety disclosure relative to capability disclosure,
- fragmented accountability across model/provider/scaffolding layers,
- unsettled norms for browser/web conduct by agents.[T19]

MIT Sloan’s 2026 guidance similarly says agentic AI is not fully ready for broad autonomous deployment, citing hallucinations and prompt-injection risk, while still projecting high transaction automation in coming years.[T20]

## Additional academic literature (arXiv + ACL/EMNLP)

Two protocol-survey papers help structure the standards landscape:

- **A Survey of AI Agent Protocols** (arXiv:2504.16736) provides taxonomy and performance dimensions (security, scalability, latency, etc.).[T35]
- **Survey of Agent Interoperability Protocols** (arXiv:2505.02279) compares MCP/ACP/A2A/ANP and proposes phased adoption patterns.[T36]

For security, AGENTVIGIL (Findings of EMNLP 2025) provides empirical evidence that indirect prompt-injection attacks can achieve high success rates in benchmark and practical settings, reinforcing that this is not only a theoretical risk.[T37]

## Harvard law/policy analysis on the “agentic web”

Harvard JOLT commentary (2026) analyzes institutional tensions between platform-controlled and protocol-mediated governance of agent behavior online, including identity, delegation, and accountability questions.[T40]

## Practical implications for R&D and education programs

Across Cornell/Harvard/MIT plus adjacent research, three patterns are consistent:

1. **Interoperability standards are becoming a primary design variable**, not an integration afterthought.[T17][T19][T35]
2. **Security and governance are lagging technical rollout**, especially around identity, delegation, and prompt injection.[T19][T20][T37]
3. **Agent ecosystems are moving toward layered specialization** (tool protocols, inter-agent protocols, UI protocols, manifest/contract layers, and discovery/identity layers).[T17][T35][T36]
