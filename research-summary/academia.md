# Academia and Education (2025–2026)

## Cornell (eCornell): curriculum trend toward full-stack agent practice

Cornell’s Agentic AI Architecture certificate is explicitly structured from:
- LLM fundamentals and hallucination behavior,
- RAG and data grounding,
- agentic workflows/protocols (including MCP),
- governance, risk, security, and human oversight. [T08]

This is notable because it mirrors the production stack sequence teams now use (model + tools + orchestration + controls), rather than treating agentic AI as prompt engineering alone. [T08][T45]

## Harvard: protocol governance framing

Harvard Library Innovation Lab and Berkman Klein frame open protocols as the strongest practical governance lever in a fast-moving ecosystem where centralized control is limited. [T09][T10]

APTT’s framing is historically internet-native: the important signal is what communities standardize, not what any single vendor ships first. [T09]

## MIT: deployment acceleration vs. safety transparency lag

The MIT AI Agent Index (2025 edition) and paper show:
- rapid release cadence (24/30 tracked systems with major 2024–2025 updates),
- autonomy increasing across categories,
- concentrated model dependencies,
- and major safety disclosure gaps (for example many systems lacking internal/third-party safety evidence). [T11][T12][T13]

MIT Sloan’s 2026 guidance aligns with this: organizations should invest in capabilities now, but expect continued human-in-the-loop constraints due to hallucination and prompt-injection risks. [T14][T15]

## Other university and research-center evidence

### Policy and law-oriented analysis

- Harvard Kennedy School Student Policy Review article highlights macro internet-traffic and governance pressure from autonomous agents, with explicit calls for new protocol and verification infrastructure. [T17]
- Harvard JOLT commentary frames the "agentic web" as an institutional design problem (platform control vs. open protocol governance), emphasizing identity, delegation, and accountability primitives. [T18]

### Technical security research trend

Recent 2025 conference/arXiv work (for example EMNLP/ACL prompt-injection research surfaced in this survey) reflects a move from toy prompt attacks to **agent workflow attacks** (web navigation, tool execution, indirect prompt injection, and black-box red-teaming). [T47]
