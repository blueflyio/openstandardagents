# Academia and Education: Agentic AI (2025-2026)

Date of synthesis: 2026-03-25

## Cornell (eCornell): curriculum structure maps directly to production agent stacks

The eCornell **Agentic AI Architecture** certificate is explicitly structured as:

1. LLM fundamentals and prompt/context engineering  
2. RAG and private data grounding  
3. AI agents, tools, memory, multi-agent workflows, and protocol concepts (including MCP)  
4. Strategy/governance/risk/ethics and responsible deployment at scale [S18]

Why this matters: Cornell is treating agentic AI as both an engineering and governance discipline, not only a model-prompting discipline.

## Harvard (Library Innovation Lab / Berkman ecosystem): protocol literacy as governance literacy

The Library Innovation Lab’s **Agent Protocols Tech Tree** (APTT) frames open protocols as a live signal of what the builder ecosystem can already agree on. [S19]  
Key argument: AI agents are easy to build as “models using tools in a loop,” therefore difficult to regulate purely top-down; protocol choices become a major governance lever. [S19]

This is a strong institutional analogy to early Internet protocol development (TCP/IP, DNS, SMTP), where interoperability norms emerged through implementation pressure before formal regulation. [S19]

## MIT AI Agent Index (2025): deployment pace ahead of safety transparency

MIT’s 2025 AI Agent Index “Further Details” describes:

- 24/30 indexed agents released or heavily updated in 2024-2025  
- clear autonomy escalation in browser and enterprise categories  
- significant safety/transparency gaps (limited disclosure of internal and third-party evaluations)  
- unresolved standards for web conduct by agents [S20]

This is one of the strongest publicly structured datasets documenting the capability-versus-governance gap in mainstream agent deployments.

## MIT Sloan guidance (published January 7, 2026): strategic importance with operational caution

MIT Sloan’s 2026 decision guidance states that agentic AI is strategically important, but “not ready for prime time” because of:

- hallucinations and execution mistakes  
- prompt injection and related hijacking risks  
- the need for human-in-the-loop guardrails in near-term enterprise operations [S21]

The same guidance expects substantial transaction automation growth over the next five years, which implies governance must mature in parallel with adoption. [S21]

## Other academic/research sources (2025-2026)

- A multi-protocol interoperability survey (MCP, ACP, A2A, ANP) provides a comparative baseline and phased adoption framing. [S22]  
- Large-scale adversarial studies and competitions in 2025-2026 (for example AGENTVIGIL and 2026 public competition work) provide empirical evidence that indirect prompt injection remains broadly successful against advanced systems. [S46][S27]

## Synthesis for academic trendline

Across Cornell/Harvard/MIT and adjacent research, the core pattern is consistent:

- **technical stack maturity is accelerating** (protocols, frameworks, deployment tooling)
- **institutional controls are still uneven** (identity, authority boundaries, transparent safety evals)
- **agent governance is becoming infrastructure-centric**, not only policy-document-centric [S18][S19][S20][S21]
