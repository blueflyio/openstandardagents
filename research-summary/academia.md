# Academia and Research Institutions (2025-2026)

Date prepared: March 29, 2026

## Cornell (eCornell): practical agent architecture + governance framing

Cornell's Agentic AI Architecture certificate is positioned as an end-to-end path from LLM basics to autonomous agents, including RAG, multi-agent workflows, and explicit governance/risk/security/human oversight modules. That combination is notable because many commercial materials still separate technical architecture from governance practice. [SRC-CORNELL-AGENTIC]

Program-level signals:

- "AI that chats" to "AI that acts" transition with tools/memory/protocols.
- Explicit mention of MCP and multi-agent handoffs.
- Dedicated strategy/governance/ethics instruction for organizational deployment. [SRC-CORNELL-AGENTIC]

## Harvard ecosystem: protocol governance as internet-infrastructure design

### Library Innovation Lab / Berkman context

Harvard's Agent Protocols Tech Tree argues that open protocols reveal what builder communities are actually standardizing in real time, analogous to early internet protocol formation. The central thesis is that agent governance will be shaped materially by protocol choices, not only by legal policy. [SRC-HARVARD-LIL-APTT]

The same source emphasizes that agent systems are relatively easy to assemble ("models + tools + loop"), making strict centralized control difficult, and therefore increasing the importance of interoperable protocol norms. [SRC-HARVARD-LIL-APTT]

### Harvard legal-policy perspective

A 2026 Harvard JOLT digest on the "agentic web" frames governance as an institutional choice between platform-controlled rules and protocol-mediated authority, emphasizing portable identity, delegation, and accountability primitives. [SRC-HARVARD-JOLT-AGENTIC-WEB]

### Harvard policy review signal on web traffic and infrastructure pressure

A Harvard Kennedy School student policy review article states that bots represent roughly 50% of internet traffic and warns that more autonomous bot-bot coordination could increase traffic pressure, requiring better coordination protocols and stronger human/bot differentiation systems. This is useful context, but should be treated as policy commentary rather than a peer-reviewed measurement study. [SRC-HKS-POLICY-REVIEW]

## MIT: deployment speed, autonomy growth, and transparency gaps

### MIT AI Agent Index 2025

The MIT AI Agent Index (and associated arXiv paper) documents 30 deployed systems and reports:

- rapid release cadence in 2024-2025,
- growing high-autonomy deployment patterns,
- substantial safety-evaluation transparency deficits,
- unsettled standards for web conduct by autonomous agents. [SRC-MIT-INDEX-2025] [SRC-MIT-INDEX-DETAILS] [SRC-MIT-INDEX-PAPER]

The "further details" page is especially important methodologically because it explains inclusion criteria, field-level annotation process, and reliance on public disclosures with limited vendor response rates. [SRC-MIT-INDEX-DETAILS]

### MIT Sloan (managerial guidance for 2026)

MIT Sloan's 2026 guidance highlights that agentic AI is not yet broadly "prime time" because of hallucinations and prompt-injection risks, while still expecting strong growth in high-volume transactional use over a five-year horizon. The recommended posture is practical: begin use-case development and internal capability-building now, but retain human oversight for high-risk execution paths. [SRC-MIT-SLOAN-2026]

## Other academic and scholarly literature

### Protocol interoperability survey literature

An arXiv survey (May 2025) compares MCP, ACP, A2A, and ANP and proposes phased adoption patterns based on architecture and deployment context. This is useful as a structured map of protocol scope, though implementers should cross-check with latest protocol specs because these projects evolve rapidly. [SRC-ARXIV-PROTOCOL-SURVEY-2025]

## Cross-academic synthesis

Across Cornell, Harvard, and MIT materials, a consistent picture emerges:

1. Agent capability growth is outpacing shared governance and measurement practice.
2. Open protocols are becoming de facto governance infrastructure.
3. Identity, delegation, and auditability are replacing "just model quality" as central control concerns.
4. Human-in-the-loop remains operationally necessary in many high-impact settings as of March 2026.
